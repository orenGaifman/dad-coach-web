# Illustration Style

## Why Illustration Matters

Illustration humanizes the product at emotional moments — onboarding, achievements, empty states, and milestone celebrations. It adds personality where UI alone feels sterile. But it must be used with extreme restraint. Too much illustration shifts the product toward childishness or app-game territory.

## Emotional Intent

### Should Create

- Warmth and emotional resonance
- Sense of personal achievement
- Gentle humanity
- Moments of delight (earned, not forced)

### Should Avoid

- Childishness or infantile associations
- Cartoon energy or visual noise
- Literal depictions that feel prescriptive
- Gendered stereotypes
- Visual weight that competes with content

## Illustration Principles

### 1. Sparse and Earned

Illustrations appear only at meaningful moments: first completion, belt achievements, onboarding welcome, and emotional touchpoints. They are never decorative background noise. Their rarity is what makes them special.

### 2. Abstract Over Literal

We do not illustrate literal scenes of fatherhood (man holding baby, family at dinner). Abstract or semi-abstract imagery avoids prescribing what a family should look like and respects the diversity of our users' situations.

Instead: shapes, organic forms, natural motifs (growth, light, warmth, connection) that *evoke* rather than *depict*.

### 3. Calm Palette

Illustrations use the product's [color system](./COLOR_SYSTEM.md) — warm, muted, earthy. Never bright, never primary, never more saturated than the UI around them.

### 4. Premium Execution

The style should feel considered and artful — closer to editorial illustration than app mascots. Quality signals that we take the subject seriously.

### 5. Inclusive by Absence

By not depicting specific people, skin tones, or family configurations, we include everyone by default. When human forms are necessary, they are stylized enough to be universal.

### 6. Scale Appropriate

Illustrations work at the size they're displayed. No complex scenes scaled down to thumbnails. Each illustration is designed for its specific context.

## Usage Contexts

| Context | Role | Scale |
|---------|------|-------|
| Onboarding | Welcome, set emotional tone | Large, prominent |
| Belt achievements | Celebrate milestone | Medium, centered |
| Empty states | Soften absence, invite action | Small-medium, supportive |
| Error/offline states | Reduce frustration | Small, gentle |
| Coaching milestones | Mark personal growth | Medium, earned |

## What We Don't Use

- Character mascots or recurring illustrated figures
- Literal family scenes with identifiable demographics
- Bright, energetic, or playful illustration styles
- Stock illustration libraries (generic clip-art quality)
- Animated illustrations as primary content (see [Motion Philosophy](./MOTION_PHILOSOPHY.md))

## Inspiration

| Reference | Illustration Lesson |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Headspace | Warm, organic, abstract — but Dad Coach is less illustrated overall |
| Slack (empty states) | Simple, purposeful, not overdone |
| Apple Fitness awards | Premium quality for achievement moments |
| Notion (minimal use) | Restraint — illustrations support, never dominate |
