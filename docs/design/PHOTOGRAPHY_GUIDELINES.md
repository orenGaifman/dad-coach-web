# Photography Guidelines

## Why Photography Matters

Photography anchors the product in reality. While illustration handles abstract emotional moments, photography grounds the experience in the real world of fatherhood — authentic, imperfect, and beautiful. It validates the father's experience by reflecting it back without idealization.

## Emotional Intent

### Should Create

- Recognition ("that looks like my life")
- Warmth and tenderness
- Authenticity and imperfection
- Quiet intimacy
- Pride without performance

### Should Avoid

- Idealization or staged perfection
- Stock photography artificiality
- Hypermasculine father stereotypes
- Poverty-tourism sentimentality
- Clinical or documentary coldness

## Photography Principles

### 1. Candid Over Posed

Real moments, not manufactured ones. A father's hand on a small back. A kitchen table with morning mess. A bedtime story captured mid-sentence. The beauty is in the imperfection.

### 2. Close and Intimate

Tight framing that emphasizes connection and detail — hands, expressions, small gestures. Not wide establishing shots that feel distant or editorial.

### 3. Natural Light

Warm, soft lighting that feels like home. Morning window light. Evening golden hour. Lamp-lit interiors. Never studio flash or harsh overhead.

### 4. Diverse Without Tokenizing

Photography naturally represents diverse family structures, ethnicities, and contexts — not as a checklist, but as the default reality of modern fatherhood.

### 5. Father-Centered

The father is always present — not as a spectator, but as participant. He's on the floor, in the mess, in the moment. Children appear in relation to him, not independently.

### 6. Muted and Warm Post-Processing

Consistent editing: slightly desaturated, warm-shifted, soft contrast. Cohesive with the product's [color system](./COLOR_SYSTEM.md). Never over-processed, filtered, or artificially moody.

## Usage Contexts

| Context | Approach |
|---------|----------|
| Marketing / Landing | Hero imagery — authentic, emotional, immediate impact |
| Onboarding | Subtle, supportive — sets context without overwhelming |
| Blog / Content | Editorial quality — storytelling through imagery |
| In-app | Minimal to none — interface stays content-focused |

## What We Don't Use

- Stock photography with visible staging
- Photos where the father looks uncomfortable or performing
- Images of "perfect" families in "perfect" homes
- Photos that imply one correct way to parent
- Heavily filtered or stylized imagery that feels inauthentic
- Photos of children alone (always in context of father-child connection)

## Subject Guidance

**Good subjects:**
- Hands together (father and child)
- Floor-level play from the father's perspective
- Quiet moments: reading, walking, waiting together
- Messy real life: dishes undone, toys scattered, hair uncombed
- Micro-expressions of joy, focus, or tenderness

**Avoid:**
- "Superdad" heroic poses
- Father looking at camera while child is prop
- Exclusively outdoor/active scenarios (respect that connection happens at home too)
- Only young babies (represent ages 0–12 as our audience serves)
