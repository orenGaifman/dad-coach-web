# Typography

## Why Typography Matters

Typography carries the voice. Before a father reads the words, the typeface communicates whether this product is for him — whether it's trustworthy, modern, and respectful of his time. In a coaching product where words are the primary interface, type is arguably the most important design decision.

## Emotional Intent

### Should Create

- Clarity and readability (especially under fatigue)
- Warmth and approachability
- Modern confidence
- Premium quality
- Calm pacing

### Should Avoid

- Coldness or sterility (geometric sans-serifs without humanist qualities)
- Playfulness or whimsy (rounded, bouncy, childlike faces)
- Density or academic weight (small, tight, information-heavy)
- Generic blandness (system defaults without character)
- Formality or stiffness (traditional serifs in body text)

## Type Strategy

### Display / Headings

Headings should feel confident but not aggressive. A modern sans-serif with subtle humanist warmth — geometric enough to feel current, organic enough to feel approachable.

Qualities:
- Medium weight for headings (not bold/heavy)
- Generous letter spacing
- Clean, contemporary character
- Works well in large sizes without feeling loud

### Body / Reading

Body text is where fathers spend most of their time — reading coaching responses, reviewing progress, processing guidance. It must be effortlessly readable even at 11 PM with tired eyes.

Qualities:
- Optimized for screen readability at 16–18px base
- Comfortable at multiple sizes without adjustment
- Generous x-height for legibility
- Open counters and clear character differentiation
- Warm personality without distraction

### Monospace / Data

Used sparingly for timestamps, numerical data, or progress metrics. Should feel clean and purposeful.

## Typographic Principles

### 1. Generous Line Height

Coaching text needs breathing room. Line height should err toward spacious (1.6–1.75 for body) to create a calm, unhurried reading experience.

### 2. Hierarchy Through Weight and Space, Not Size

Minimize the number of font sizes. Create hierarchy through weight variation and spatial relationships. Three to four sizes maximum across the entire product.

### 3. Left-Aligned, Natural Flow

All text left-aligned. No centered body text. Reading should follow natural eye movement without extra cognitive work.

### 4. Responsive Comfort

Type should feel equally comfortable on a phone held in one hand and on a tablet propped at arm's length. Size choices should respect the context where fathers actually use the product.

### 5. Limit Uppercase

Uppercase text feels like shouting. Use it only for small labels or metadata — never for headings, buttons, or coaching content.

## Scale Guidance

- **Base body:** Comfortably readable without squinting (16–18px range)
- **Small / secondary:** Metadata, timestamps — clearly secondary (13–14px)
- **Headings:** Clear hierarchy, never overwhelming (20–28px range)
- **Display:** Used rarely — onboarding, achievements, emotional moments (32–40px)
