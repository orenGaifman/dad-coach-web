# Personas

These personas represent the primary users of Dad Coach. They are fictional but grounded in real patterns observed in the [target audience](./TARGET_AUDIENCE.md).

---

## Persona 1: Amit — The Overwhelmed New Dad

> "I want to be the kind of dad she runs to, not away from — I just don't know what I'm doing yet."

### Background

- Age: 32
- Occupation: Software engineer at a mid-size tech company
- Location: Central Israel
- Education: B.Sc. Computer Science

### Family Situation

- Married, first child (daughter, 8 months old)
- Wife returned to work; both share parenting responsibilities
- Extended family nearby but advice often feels outdated

### Typical Weekday

- 6:30 — Wakes to baby crying. Handles morning feed while wife showers.
- 7:15 — Hands off to wife, showers, grabs coffee.
- 8:30–17:30 — Work. Checks baby cam once or twice. Googles "8 month old not crawling" during lunch.
- 18:00 — Arrives home. Takes over for bath time.
- 19:00 — Bedtime routine. Sometimes smooth, sometimes a disaster.
- 19:45–21:30 — Dinner with wife. Debrief on the day. Occasionally a show.
- 21:30 — Scrolls phone. Feels vaguely guilty he's not reading a parenting book.

### Daily Challenges

- Doesn't know what's "normal" for his daughter's development
- Feels clumsy during play — unsure what activities are appropriate for her age
- Wife seems to intuitively know what to do; he feels a step behind
- Sleep deprivation eroding patience and presence
- Conflicting advice from family and internet

### Technology Comfort

High. Uses apps for fitness, finance, and productivity. Expects clean, modern interfaces. Will abandon anything that feels clunky or patronizing.

### Biggest Fear

That the early months will pass and he'll realize he was present but not *connected* — that he went through the motions without building a real bond.

### Biggest Success Scenario

His daughter lights up when he walks in the room. He has his own rituals with her that are distinctly *theirs*. His wife trusts him completely with solo parenting. He feels like an equal parent, not a backup.

### What Success Looks Like After One Year

Amit has a confident morning routine with his daughter. He understands her developmental stage without anxiety. He has 3–4 go-to activities for different moods and energy levels. He handles solo days without stress. His wife tells him he's a great dad — and he believes it.

## Persona 2: David — The Stretched-Thin Father of Three

> "I love my kids more than anything, but by the time I get home, I'm running on empty."

### Background

- Age: 41
- Occupation: Regional sales manager
- Location: Suburbs, commutes 45 minutes each way
- Education: MBA

### Family Situation

- Married, three children (ages 3, 7, and 10)
- Wife works part-time; primary logistics coordinator for the family
- Eldest child showing behavioral challenges at school

### Typical Weekday

- 6:00 — Alarm. Showers in silence. Only quiet moment of the day.
- 6:45 — Out the door. Sometimes sees the kids, often doesn't.
- 7:00–18:00 — Work. Back-to-back calls. Occasional school email that spikes his anxiety.
- 18:30 — Home. Kids are mid-homework/dinner chaos. Jumps in.
- 19:00 — Homework battles with eldest. Feels like he's policing, not parenting.
- 20:00 — Younger two need bedtime routine. Reads a book, half-present.
- 21:00 — Finally sits. Wife debriefs on logistics. Falls asleep on the couch.

### Daily Challenges

- Each child needs different things at different stages
- Eldest son is pulling away — conversations feel forced
- Defaults to discipline over connection when stressed
- Guilt about work travel and missed school events
- Wife carries the mental load; he feels like he's always catching up

### Technology Comfort

Moderate. Uses work tools and WhatsApp extensively. Doesn't explore new apps unless clearly valuable and low-friction.

### Biggest Fear

That his eldest will remember him as the angry, distracted dad who was always on his phone or at work — and that by the time he notices, it will be too late to repair.

### Biggest Success Scenario

His eldest son comes to him with a problem voluntarily. Each child has a unique tradition with him. He's patient during conflict. His wife says "the kids are different when you're around" — meaning calmer, not more anxious.

### What Success Looks Like After One Year

David has a weekly one-on-one ritual with each child (even 15 minutes). He's rebuilt connection with his eldest through shared activities they both enjoy. He responds to misbehavior with curiosity before correction. He no longer dreads evenings — he has a plan that works for his energy level.

## Persona 3: Yoni — The Intentional First-Timer

> "I've designed products for millions of users. How hard can one baby be? ...I'm terrified."

### Background

- Age: 29
- Occupation: UX designer at a startup
- Location: Urban apartment
- Education: B.Des. Visual Communication

### Family Situation

- Partner is 7 months pregnant with their first child
- Unmarried but committed and cohabitating
- Both sets of parents live abroad — no local family support

### Typical Weekday

- 8:30 — Wakes naturally. Makes coffee. Checks messages.
- 9:00–18:00 — Remote work. Deep focus periods mixed with baby-prep tasks.
- 18:00 — Evening walk with partner. Talks about baby logistics and fears.
- 19:30 — Dinner. Reads parenting articles or watches YouTube explainers.
- 21:00 — Assembles baby furniture or researches products.
- 22:30 — Lies awake thinking about whether he's ready.

### Daily Challenges

- Information overload — can't distinguish good advice from noise
- Anxiety about doing things "right" from day one
- No local support network; friends haven't had kids yet
- Partner's expectations feel high and undefined
- The gap between theoretical preparation and lived reality looms large

### Technology Comfort

Very high. Early adopter. Appreciates thoughtful design, personalization, and products that respect his intelligence.

### Biggest Fear

That all his preparation won't matter — that the newborn reality will overwhelm him and he'll freeze, withdraw, or become the stressed, unhelpful partner he's seen in online forums.

### Biggest Success Scenario

The first weeks are hard but navigable. He feels prepared enough to be useful, flexible enough to adapt. His partner sees him as a true co-parent from day one. He trusts his instincts alongside the guidance.

### What Success Looks Like After One Year

Yoni has found his parenting rhythm without needing to research every decision. He's bonded with his baby on his own terms. He and his partner co-parent as a team without resentment. He's moved from anxious preparation to confident presence. He recommends Dad Coach to expecting friends.
