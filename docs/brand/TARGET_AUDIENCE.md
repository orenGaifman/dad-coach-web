# Target Audience

## Primary Audience

**Fathers aged 28–45 with children aged 0–12**

These are men who are actively involved in parenting and want to do better. They may be first-time dads feeling overwhelmed, or experienced fathers looking to deepen their connection with their kids. They value self-improvement and are willing to invest time — if it's efficient and practical.

### Characteristics

- Employed full-time, often in demanding roles
- Digitally comfortable (use apps, messaging, smartphones daily)
- Value efficiency — limited patience for fluff
- Want actionable advice, not theory
- May feel isolated in their parenting challenges
- Compare themselves to their own fathers (often wanting to do more/better)

## Secondary Audience

- **Expectant fathers** preparing for their first child
- **Father figures** (stepfathers, uncles, grandfathers in parenting roles)
- **Co-parents** looking to align on parenting approaches

## Pain Points

- Lack of structured support specifically for fathers
- Feeling unprepared for developmental stages
- Difficulty balancing work demands with family presence
- Not knowing how to connect with children at different ages
- Guilt about not being present enough
- Conflicting advice from family, internet, and social circles
- Reluctance to ask for help or admit struggles

## Goals

- Build stronger, more meaningful relationships with their children
- Feel confident in parenting decisions
- Be more present and intentional during family time
- Develop consistent routines and habits
- Understand child development stages and respond appropriately
- Break negative patterns from their own upbringing

## Motivations

- Love for their children and desire to give them the best start
- Personal growth and self-improvement drive
- Wanting to be remembered as a great dad
- Creating family memories and traditions
- Being a positive role model

## Frustrations

- Generic parenting content that doesn't speak to fathers
- Advice that assumes unlimited time and energy
- Judgment from partners, family, or society
- Apps that feel clinical, feminine-coded, or preachy
- Information overload without clear next steps
- Lack of progress visibility in parenting
